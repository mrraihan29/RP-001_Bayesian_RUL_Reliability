# Comparator fairness and evaluation audit (v0.4)

**Status:** Prospective audit of the frozen primary comparison, not an evaluation result. Primary comparison remains the Bayesian principal procedure versus calibrated CQR on mean 90% interval score. Official test sensors and labels remain unauthorized; no test prediction or score exists. The comparator contract is defined by the [v0.3 comparative design](../v0.3/06_comparative_evaluation_design.md), [CQR implementation contract](../v0.3/comparator_implementation_contract.md), [v0.4 owner directive](owner_directive.md), and [frozen v0.4 configuration](../../configs/v0.4/remediation_plan.json).

## Comparison being made

The primary contrast compares two complete prediction procedures under the same endpoint, uncapped RUL target, mean 90% interval score, and reserved engine-role design. It does not isolate a causal effect of “Bayesian inference.” A joint generative Gaussian hierarchy with posterior integration differs from quantile regression with conformal interval expansion in model assumptions, regularization, representation, and interval construction. Any observed finite-benchmark difference belongs to those complete procedures as implemented.

The primary Bayesian output is its principal posterior predictive interval. CQR is calibrated by a nonnegative conformal expansion. That means the primary comparison intentionally contrasts an unexpanded Bayesian interval with calibrated CQR; the score difference can reflect calibration and interval construction as well as the model family. The secondary Bayesian-plus-post-hoc-conformal analysis can describe how the same expansion rule changes Bayesian intervals, but it is an exploratory ablation. It cannot replace the primary comparison or establish that Bayesian inference itself caused an advantage.

## Information access and representation

Both methods use the same frozen role split and training-only eligible cohorts, PCA fitted from eligible fit prefixes only, labels for fit and tune engines, and sensor information available through each engine's single assigned cutoff. The target is uncapped RUL. Neither method may use post-cutoff sensors, calibration labels during candidate selection, or statistics pooled from the official test population. Each held-out endpoint is processed using its own observed prefix; other test engines do not contribute to a global sensor update or to model selection. These boundaries provide equal access to allowed engine-level information, not identical model inputs or inductive biases.

The fixed feature maps differ:

| Procedure | Representation from the observed prefix | Consequence for attribution |
|---|---|---|
| Bayesian principal | The observed PC sequence in the fixed window, together with engine age and the hierarchical latent trajectory. Sensor information updates that engine's predictive distribution. | Uses ordered sequence information through the joint model and its prior/covariance assumptions. |
| CQR gradient boosting | Five fixed summaries: age, observed endpoint PC, OLS level, OLS slope, and residual standard deviation. | Compresses the sequence to summaries, which can discard local temporal structure. |
| CQR linear quantile regression | Age plus the fixed 30-position observed PC sequence. | Uses the ordered window in a linear quantile model with a different regularization and shape assumption. |

These are the planned v0.3 representations and are retained for v0.4; no matched-representation control is added. Thus equal data availability does not imply equal feature information after transformation. The training-only eligibility audit found 0 exact common prefixes and 0 near-duplicate candidates under its stated heuristic, but that audit does not prove independence or transport to the official test cohort ([split and prefix audit](../v0.3/04_dataset_split_eligibility_cutoff_audit.md)).

The cohort design itself also limits what “same data” means. Pseudo-cutoff eligibility is C < T, so each method is trained and calibrated on survivors to assigned cutoffs. In the historical 100-salt membership audit, total eligible count varied from 69 to 85, calibration count from 18 to 29, and 83 IDs changed eligibility in at least one scenario. Cutoff-lifetime association was descriptive and differed between all assigned engines and survivors. These findings motivate the full-stage perturbation but do not establish official cutoff exchangeability; details and corrected conformal ranks are in the [estimand and cutoff assessment](05_estimand_cutoff_transport.md).

## Selection, prior policy, and unequal search budgets

CQR selection is limited to the predeclared 12-candidate grid: eight gradient-boosting configurations and four linear quantile-regression configurations. Selection uses the eligible tune engines and the mean raw, uncalibrated 90% interval score. Scores within the predeclared 0.5-cycle tie tolerance are treated as tied, with the fixed linear-model preference and then the simpler candidate rule resolving ties. The selected v0.3 development candidate was gradient boosting with depth 1, minimum leaf size 10, and learning rate 0.1. This is a selection tolerance only; it is not a practical-superiority margin, an effect threshold, or a test of statistical significance. In particular, it does not revive the rejected five-cycle practical margin.

The Bayesian procedure has no interval-score search over prior variants. The v0.4 principal policy uses the anchored_v04 prior fixed before new predictions; it is a declared development adaptation after v0.3 calibration outcomes had already been inspected. The plan states that its prior alternatives are sensitivity-only and cannot select a winner. The frozen sensitivity policies are a 1.5-times broader prior, a zero-autocorrelation alternative, and a contamination-error alternative. Three independent principal fits with four chains and 8,000 retained draws per fit assess numerical precision; the sensitivity fits and three fixed sensor-oracle checks serve their named precision or robustness purposes, not model selection. The broader research budget also includes synthetic and eight pipeline perturbation fits. The machine-readable plan caps the full v0.4 program at 33 scientific MCMC fits and freezes seeds and run purposes; this is not computational parity with CQR's 12 tuning candidates.

These budgets are materially different and must be reported as such. CQR has 12 candidates selected on 13 eligible tune engines; Bayesian has one principal prior policy and no score-selected prior search. The small tune cohort makes the chosen CQR configuration uncertain. Neither the fixed Bayesian policy nor the single CQR winner should be described as having had equal hyperparameter search freedom. No grid expansion, winner substitution, or post-test tuning is permitted. Preserve every candidate record, seed, failure, and selection rule ([CQR contract](../v0.3/comparator_implementation_contract.md); [v0.4 prospective plan](prospective_remediation_plan.md); [configuration](../../configs/v0.4/remediation_plan.json)).

## Calibration, exposure, and secondary Bayesian ablation

CQR's correction is computed after candidate selection and refitting using the reserved calibration cohort. For nominal 90% coverage the finite rank is k = ceil(0.9(n+1)); with the canonical n = 25, k = 24. The correction is q = max(0, s_(k)), so it can only expand the interval. Historically, the selected CQR candidate had q = 19.987559 cycles on that development cohort. This is a training-only calibration result, not independent coverage evidence.

The v0.3 freeze record says calibration outcomes were not used before the CQR selection freeze, but those outcomes were subsequently inspected in calibration and influence analyses. The Bayesian prior and other method policies were also changed in the v0.4 development plan after that exposure. Consequently, these outcomes cannot be called untouched validation data, and any v0.4 refit, reselection, correction, or sensitivity that uses them is exploratory development. Recomputing a calibration quantile does not erase prior exposure or make the cohort an independent confirmatory set. This limitation is recorded in the [v0.3 calibration report](../v0.3/05_calibration_feasibility_precision_report.md), [selection freeze](../../configs/pilot_selection_freeze.json), and [owner decision](../v0.3/owner_decision.md).

Bayesian plus post-hoc calibration is secondary only. It applies the same nonnegative rule q = max(0, s_(k)) to a frozen Bayesian predictive interval, using the same calibration cohort under the declared use policy. The historical correction was q = 0. This does not prove raw Bayesian coverage or confer an official exchangeability guarantee; the v0.3 numerical predictive-precision gate also failed. The v0.4 full-pipeline resamples contain duplicate calibration IDs as multiplicities, not new exchangeable engines, so no formal conformal guarantee is claimed for those perturbations. Do not promote this ablation to primary status based on any observed result.

## Pipeline perturbations and interpretation

The frozen v0.4 plan varies engine resampling and cutoff realization in four paired bootstrap seeds, each with canonical and alternate cutoff variants. Each replicate repeats PCA, the 12-candidate CQR selection, refitting, Bayesian fitting, and calibration. Engine resampling is stratified within the fixed fit/tune/calibration roles; duplicate IDs carry multiplicity. A single cutoff is assigned to each unique engine in a replicate, eligibility remains C < T with no redraw, and a run below the distinct-eligible minimum is recorded as a failure.

This gives eight bounded empirical perturbations of several stages together. They are not eight independent evaluations, do not estimate the full sampling distribution of the pipeline, and cannot support a precise variance decomposition. The fixed 25 original calibration sensor prefixes are descriptive prediction-map anchors only. Their RUL values are prohibited from selecting priors or algorithms, and they do not form an independent assessment set. No scientific perturbation run or Bayesian fit is performed in this audit.

## Failures and reporting policy

Report the exact finite-benchmark paired mean only if every planned endpoint has both finite method scores and an authorized label. A missing prediction, NaN, overflow, infinite interval score, or absent pair remains attached to its engine ID and failure reason. Do not drop engines, impute or cap scores, winsorize, or substitute a different model or inferential method after results. Under the frozen inference policy, an incomplete or nonfinite score vector makes the primary numerical comparison unavailable; an invalid or degenerate paired bootstrap-t diagnostic is suppressed and reported with all failure counts. It is not replaced by a favorable test. The complete policy is in the [inference worker contract](worker_inference_contract.md).

## Fairness disposition

- **Allowed training information — documented and shared.** Same role assignments, cutoff-limited sensor availability, training targets, and PCA fit boundary; feature transformations and model assumptions remain different and are explicit.
- **Selection and compute budgets — documented, unequal by design.** Report 12 CQR candidates on 13 tune engines, one fixed Bayesian principal prior, its three nonselecting sensitivities, and the frozen overall run cap. Do not imply matched search or compute.
- **Calibration exposure — STILL BLOCKED for confirmatory interpretation.** The historical development cohort has been inspected; v0.4 outputs using it remain exploratory.
- **Attribution to Bayesian inference alone — MITIGATED BY CLAIM RESTRICTION.** Interpret any eventual primary contrast as a comparison of the specified complete procedures, not as an isolated causal benefit of Bayesian inference.
- **Official benchmark and real-engine results — unavailable.** Test access and confirmation remain unauthorized; no score, superiority, or reliability result is reported here.
