## Checks that survived review

The structural inversion is valid under the report’s stated assumptions: full-rank age design and B, w>=6, positive sigma_z, positive-definite S, and |rho|<1. For second-difference row lag h>=2, c_h=sigma_z² rho^(h-2)(1-rho)^4; c₂ is positive and c₃/c₂ recovers rho, including rho=0. The qualification is that h=2 is overlapping as noted above. Recovery of S, gamma, sigma_r², and beta then follows from the stated left-inverse and cross-moment equations. The report appropriately distinguishes this population-moment identification result from finite-sample precision and physical interpretation.

The analytic prior variance for log RUL is correct under the independent zero-mean priors stated in the report. It includes variance from beta, the product gamma′Gamma a, the random latent deviation, and response noise. The endpoint sensor variance expression is also consistent with the specified basis and independent sensor noise.

The self-normalized mixture-quantile influence, chain-wise long-run variance, density conversion, and oracle weighting are mathematically coherent under stationarity/mixing, a ratio CLT, positive density, and adequate moment conditions. The report explicitly limits the Satterthwaite guard to an approximation rather than a finite-sample guarantee. Adding a sensor likelihood in an oracle fit and then predicting with reweighting disabled avoids counting that sensor likelihood twice.

The estimand and failure language is appropriately bounded: the complete finite benchmark contrast is an enumerated stored-prediction effect; a bootstrap conditional on one realized pipeline does not estimate total pipeline uncertainty; conformal marginal exchangeability, conditional cohort coverage, and real-engine transport remain separate assumptions. The stated suppression of secondary bootstrap diagnostics for missing/nonfinite scores or invalid pivots avoids dropping difficult outcomes. I found no mathematical basis to broaden those claims.
